# Crypto Scam Evidence Guide

An educational resource for organizing evidence after a suspected cryptocurrency scam.

This repository is designed to help people document cryptocurrency transactions and related communications in a structured way.

It does not provide guaranteed recovery services and does not claim that cryptocurrency can always be recovered.

## Why Evidence Matters

After a cryptocurrency scam, people often have information scattered across screenshots, wallet applications, exchanges, emails, messaging platforms, and blockchain explorers.

Organizing that information can make the incident easier to understand and report.

The first objective should be documentation.

## What to Record

### Transaction Information

Record:

* Blockchain network
* Transaction ID or TXID
* Sending address
* Receiving address
* Amount
* Transaction date and time
* Confirmation status
* Relevant subsequent transactions

### Communication Information

Preserve:

* Emails
* Messages
* Usernames
* Website URLs
* Social-media profiles
* Phone numbers
* Payment instructions
* Invoices
* Screenshots

Keep original evidence whenever possible.

## Suggested Evidence Timeline

A simple timeline can be structured like this:

| Date       | Event               | Transaction ID | Amount | Notes                |
| ---------- | ------------------- | -------------- | -----: | -------------------- |
| YYYY-MM-DD | Initial contact     | —              |      — | Describe the event   |
| YYYY-MM-DD | Payment requested   | —              |      — | Describe the request |
| YYYY-MM-DD | Cryptocurrency sent | TXID           | 0.0000 | Record transaction   |
| YYYY-MM-DD | Follow-up request   | —              |      — | Record communication |

The purpose of the timeline is to separate verified events from assumptions.

## Blockchain Verification

A blockchain explorer can provide information about transactions recorded on a public blockchain.

Depending on the network, available information may include:

* Transaction status
* Block confirmation
* Sender and recipient addresses
* Amount transferred
* Transaction timestamp
* Subsequent transfers

Blockchain records can help establish where funds moved.

They do not automatically establish the real-world identity of the person controlling an address.

## Recovery Scam Warning

People who have already lost cryptocurrency may become targets for additional scams.

Be cautious about anyone claiming:

* Guaranteed recovery
* Guaranteed refunds
* Guaranteed access to frozen funds
* Blockchain release fees
* Wallet activation fees
* Mandatory cryptocurrency payments
* Requests for seed phrases or private keys

Never disclose a wallet's private key or seed phrase to someone claiming they need it to investigate a transaction.

## Reporting

Depending on the circumstances, relevant reporting options may include:

* The cryptocurrency exchange involved
* Financial institutions
* Law enforcement
* Consumer-protection agencies
* Appropriate regulatory or reporting organizations

Provide factual information that can be independently verified.

Avoid presenting assumptions as established facts.

Before submitting a report, check whether you have preserved:
